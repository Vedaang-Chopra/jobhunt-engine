# Role Fit, Difficulty, and Application Priority Rules

## Critical rule
Fit and attainability are different.

A role can be technically exciting but unrealistic today. A less prestigious role can be substantially more likely to produce an interview and deserve higher immediate priority.

## Evaluate every serious role on
### Role Fit
Match between actual work and demonstrated profile.

### Attainability / Interview Probability
Likelihood of passing recruiter and hiring-team screening with the current profile.

### Profile Gap
Important missing or weakly demonstrated requirements.

### Strategic Value
Technical quality, career upside, research exposure, team/company value, future positioning.

### Connection Strength
Referral, alumni, mutual, recruiter, team, or hiring-manager path.

### Location Fit
Alignment with geographic preferences.

### Urgency
Posting freshness, closing risk, hiring-post recency, direct team hiring signals.

### Overall Application Priority
Reasoned synthesis of all dimensions.

## Two different kinds of difficulty
- `Company bar`: the company/team is extremely selective.
- `Profile mismatch`: the role expects capabilities the profile does not currently demonstrate.

Record them separately.

## Default labels
- Strong / Higher-Probability
- Realistic
- Competitive
- Stretch
- Poor Current Fit

## Suggested scoring guidance
0–100 application priority:
- role/technical fit: 30%
- attainability: 25%
- strategic value: 15%
- location: 10%
- connection strength: 10%
- urgency/freshness: 10%

This is guidance, not blind arithmetic. The LLM may override a mechanical score when it records a reason.

## Portfolio rule
Do not spend all early applications on elite/stretch labs. Maintain a mix of higher-probability, realistic competitive, high-value stretch, and selected elite opportunities.
