# LinkedIn Referral and Connection Research Rules

## Goal
Given a company or role, find people who provide a credible path to referral, introduction, hiring-team insight, or useful conversation. Do not dump the first LinkedIn employees returned.

## Search hierarchy
1. 1st-degree connections.
2. 2nd-degree connections.
3. 3rd-degree connections.
4. Active posters about hiring (see "Active hiring posters" below).
5. Georgia Tech alumni.
6. Former coworkers/shared employers.
7. Mutual connections.
8. People in the relevant technical domain.
9. People on the relevant team.
10. Hiring managers/team leads.
11. Recruiters tied to the organization.
12. People with strong shared research/project context.

## People-search filter rule
Use LinkedIn's native people-search filters instead of bare keyword queries:
- **Connection degree** filter (1st, then 2nd) — run one pass per degree tier;
- **Current company** filter to pin results to the target company;
- **Location** filter (US / Remote-US first; other regions only when the
  relationship case is strong);
- **Keywords** facet for role/skill terms when offered.

Navigate the UI like an agent: open Search → People, read the active filter
chips and result count after each change, verify each tier's filters actually
registered before collecting names, and paginate rather than trusting the
first page. Record which filters were applied in the contact-research output.

## Active hiring posters (high-priority signal)
Beyond connection degree, actively identify people at the target company who
regularly post hiring-related content on LinkedIn: "we're hiring", "ask me for
referrals", "connect with me", hiring posts for open roles, team shout-outs
with open positions, and similar. LinkedIn activity level is a first-class
ranking signal — an active poster who invites outreach is often a better
referral path than a higher-degree connection who never posts.

For every serious contact search:
- search the company's recent posts for hiring/referral language and note who
  authors them;
- check each candidate's recent activity (posting frequency, hiring-related
  posts) during profile inspection;
- record their activity level and any observed hiring posts in the contact
  record (add to "shared context"/"reason to contact" and the recommended
  ask, e.g. "responded to their referral post");
- prioritize active posters in outreach order when relevance is comparable.

## Geography
For U.S. roles, prefer U.S.-based people when all else is equal. Strong contacts in India, Toronto, Europe, or elsewhere remain valuable if relationship/alumni/mutual/internal referral strength is better.

## Individual profile inspection is mandatory
For every serious contact candidate:
- inspect the profile;
- confirm current company/role/team;
- identify shared context;
- assess relevance to the target role;
- decide whether referral, introduction, informational outreach, or no outreach is appropriate;
- record why the person is worth contacting.

LinkedIn ranking alone is not evidence.

## Ranking signals
Connection degree, alumni link, mutuals, shared employers, technical/domain overlap, same team/org, hiring-manager/recruiter relevance, geographic relevance, relationship strength, ability to refer, quality of the reason for outreach, and LinkedIn activity level (posting frequency and hiring/referral posts — active posters rank above inactive contacts of comparable relevance).

## Required record fields
Name, LinkedIn URL, company, title, location, degree, relevant team, alumni/shared affiliation, mutuals, shared context, target job IDs, reason to contact, recommended ask, referral likelihood, outreach priority, email/status/source, outreach status, last verified.
